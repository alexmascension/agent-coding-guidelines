## Shared across guides

### Pipelines and QC aggregation

- **[Ewels 2016]** Ewels et al. (2016). MultiQC: summarize analysis results for multiple tools and samples in a single report. *Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btw354](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btw354)  
  MultiQC aggregates quality-control metrics from multiple bioinformatics tools across many samples into a single report, enabling rapid detection of batch effects and outlier samples.
- **[Ewels 2020]** Ewels et al. (2020). The nf-core framework for community-curated bioinformatics pipelines. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/s41587-020-0439-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-020-0439-x)  
  nf-core: community-curated Nextflow pipelines with shared standards, continuous testing and versioned releases; run a pinned release, not the development branch.

### File formats

- **[Bonfield 2022]** Bonfield (2022). CRAM 3.1: advances in the CRAM file format. *Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btac010](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btac010)  
  CRAM 3.1 adds codecs (rANS Nx16, adaptive arithmetic coder, fqzcomp quality model, name tokeniser) to CRAM 3.0 and is 7–16 % smaller at an equivalent profile, with similar speed except decoding of the `small` and `archive` profiles; the `archive` profile trades CPU and memory for size.

## Bulk RNA-seq (`RNASEQ_BULK_GUIDELINES.md`)

### Reviews, workflows and ecosystems

- **[Chen 2016]** Chen et al. (2016). From reads to genes to pathways: differential expression analysis of RNA-Seq experiments using Rsubread and the edgeR quasi-likelihood pipeline. *F1000Research*. [doi:10.12688/f1000research.8987.2](https://doi.org/10.12688/f1000research.8987.2)  
  This workflow combines the Rsubread aligner/quantifier with edgeR's quasi-likelihood pipeline to guide RNA-seq analysis from raw reads through differential expression to pathway analysis.
- **[Conesa 2016]** Conesa et al. (2016). A survey of best practices for RNA-seq data analysis. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-016-0881-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-016-0881-8)  
  No single RNA-seq pipeline fits all applications; researchers should select and combine methods for alignment, quantification, differential expression, splicing, fusion detection, and eQTL mapping based on specific experimental goals and challenges.
- **[Love 2016a]** Love et al. (2016). RNA-Seq workflow: gene-level exploratory analysis and differential expression. *F1000Research*. [doi:10.12688/f1000research.7035.2](https://doi.org/10.12688/f1000research.7035.2)  
  This workflow demonstrates an end-to-end Bioconductor pipeline—from FASTQ alignment through count matrices—for gene-level RNA-Seq exploratory analysis, quality assessment, and differential expression visualization.
- **[Love 2018]** Love et al. (2018). Swimming downstream: statistical analysis of differential transcript usage following Salmon quantification. *F1000Research*. [doi:10.12688/f1000research.15398.2](https://doi.org/10.12688/f1000research.15398.2)  
  Workflow for differential transcript usage after Salmon quantification (tximport, DRIMSeq/DEXSeq, stage-wise testing with stageR).

### Alignment and quantification

- **[Dobin 2012]** Dobin et al. (2012). STAR: ultrafast universal RNA-seq aligner. *Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/bts635](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/bts635)  
  STAR aligns RNA-seq reads using sequential maximum mappable seed search in suffix arrays, achieving >50-fold faster mapping speed than other aligners while improving splice-junction detection sensitivity and precision.
- **[Zhao 2015]** Zhao & Zhang (2015). A comprehensive evaluation of ensembl, RefSeq, and UCSC annotations in the context of RNA-seq read mapping and gene quantification. *BMC Genomics*. [doi:10.1186/s12864-015-1308-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-015-1308-8)  
  Gene model choice strongly affects RNA-seq quantification: comparing RefGene and Ensembl, only 16.3% of genes had identical counts, 28.1% differed by ≥5%, and 9.3% (~2038 genes) differed by ≥50%. Junction reads are most sensitive—only 53% map identically across annotations, versus 95% for non-junction reads, with 30% unalignable without gene-model guidance.
- **[Bray 2016]** Bray et al. (2016). Near-optimal probabilistic RNA-seq quantification. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/nbt.3519](https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt.3519)  
  Kallisto pseudoaligns RNA-seq reads to a reference transcriptome for quantification, achieving accuracy similar to standard methods while running two orders of magnitude faster.
- **[Love 2016b]** Love et al. (2016). Modeling of RNA-seq fragment sequence bias reduces systematic errors in transcript abundance estimation. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/nbt.3682](https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt.3682)  
  Alpine, which models fragment GC-content and sequence bias, reduces false-positive transcript-abundance calls fourfold compared with Cufflinks while maintaining true-positive detection, showing that unmodeled fragment sequence bias is a major source of systematic errors and isoform misidentification in RNA-seq quantification.
- **[Soneson 2016a]** Soneson et al. (2016). Differential analyses for RNA-seq: transcript-level estimates improve gene-level inferences. *F1000Research*. [doi:10.12688/f1000research.7563.2](https://doi.org/10.12688/f1000research.7563.2)  
  Using transcript-level abundance estimates with offsets (via the tximport R package) to generate gene-level counts improves differential gene expression inference and prevents inflated false discovery rates from isoform usage changes.
- **[Teng 2016]** Teng et al. (2016). A benchmark for RNA-seq quantification pipelines. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-016-0940-1](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-016-0940-1)  
  Using the rnaseqcomp R/Bioconductor package, seven RNA-seq quantification pipelines were benchmarked on two independent datasets; overall performance was generally poor, two methods clearly underperformed, and RSEM slightly outperformed the others.
- **[Patro 2017]** Patro et al. (2017). Salmon provides fast and bias-aware quantification of transcript expression. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/nmeth.4197](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.4197)  
  Salmon is a lightweight RNA-seq transcript quantification tool that uniquely corrects for fragment GC-content bias, improving abundance estimate accuracy and differential expression sensitivity.
- **[Zhang 2017]** Zhang et al. (2017). Evaluation and comparison of computational tools for RNA-seq isoform quantification. *BMC Genomics*. [doi:10.1186/s12864-017-4002-1](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-017-4002-1)  
  Recently developed alignment-free tools (e.g., kallisto/Salmon-type methods) were both fast and accurate for transcript-level quantification; accuracy declined with complex gene structures and short transcripts, and TP53 simulations showed sequencing depth and relative isoform abundance jointly affect quantification accuracy.
- **[Zhu 2019]** Zhu et al. (2019). Nonparametric expression analysis using inferential replicate counts. *Nucleic Acids Research*. [doi:10.1093/nar/gkz622](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkz622)  
  Swish, a nonparametric extension of SAMseq incorporating inferential replicate counts, controls false discovery rate better than existing methods, especially for transcripts with high inferential uncertainty, where methods ignoring such uncertainty show inflated FDR; Swish also outperformed the Wilcoxon test in single-cell differential expression comparisons.
- **[Srivastava 2020]** Srivastava et al. (2020). Alignment and mapping methodology influence transcript abundance estimation. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-020-02151-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-020-02151-8)  
  Selective alignment improves transcript quantification and downstream differential expression accuracy over lightweight mapping methods, without traditional alignment's computational cost, by better addressing spurious mapping artifacts.
- **[Baldoni 2023]** Baldoni et al. (2023). Dividing out quantification uncertainty allows efficient assessment of differential transcript expression with edgeR. *Nucleic Acids Research*. [doi:10.1093/nar/gkad1167](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkad1167)  
  Fitting a quasi-Poisson model to kallisto/Salmon bootstrap counts estimates read-to-transcript ambiguity overdispersion per transcript, which can be divided out to produce scaled counts; edgeR analysis of these scaled counts outperforms existing differential transcript expression pipelines in power and efficiency while correctly controlling false discovery rate.
- **[Baldoni 2025]** Baldoni et al. (2025). Dividing out quantification uncertainty enables assessment of differential transcript usage with limma and edgeR. *Nucleic Acids Research*. [doi:10.1093/nar/gkaf1305](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkaf1305)  
  Dividing isoform counts by estimated quantification uncertainty (RTA overdispersion) before applying limma or edgeR's diffSplice improves DTU detection: this approach yields greater statistical power, better computational efficiency, and improved false discovery rate control than existing specialized DTU methods, especially in small-sample-size studies.

### Normalization, features and visualization

- **[Anders 2010]** Anders & Huber (2010). Differential expression analysis for sequence count data. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/gb-2010-11-10-r106](https://doi.org/10.1186/gb-2010-11-10-r106)  
  DESeq models RNA-Seq (or similar count) data using a negative binomial distribution with variance and mean linked via local regression, enabling robust differential expression testing across the dynamic range.
- **[Robinson 2010]** Robinson & Oshlack (2010). A scaling normalization method for differential expression analysis of RNA-seq data. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/gb-2010-11-3-r25](https://doi.org/10.1186/gb-2010-11-3-r25)  
  This paper introduces the TMM (trimmed mean of M-values) scaling normalization method for RNA-seq data, which markedly improves differential expression inference accuracy compared to unnormalized analyses.
- **[Evans 2017]** Evans et al. (2017). Selecting between-sample RNA-Seq normalization methods from the perspective of their assumptions. *Briefings in Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bib/bbx008](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbx008)  
  Researchers should select RNA-Seq normalization methods based on whether their underlying assumptions hold for the given dataset, since violated assumptions degrade performance and bias downstream differential expression results.
- **[Zhao 2020]** Zhao et al. (2020). Misuse of RPKM or TPM normalization when comparing across samples and sequencing protocols. *RNA*. [doi:10.1261/rna.074922.120](https://doi.org/10.1261/rna.074922.120)  
  RPKM and TPM values reflect within-sample relative abundance and are not directly comparable across samples or sequencing protocols when RNA composition differs, so such comparisons should be avoided.

### Design, hidden factors and differential expression

- **[Leek 2007]** Leek & Storey (2007). Capturing Heterogeneity in Gene Expression Studies by Surrogate Variable Analysis. *PLoS Genetics*. [doi:10.1371/journal.pgen.0030161](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.0030161)  
  Surrogate variable analysis (SVA) identifies and adjusts for unmeasured heterogeneity in gene expression data, improving power, reducing spurious signal, and increasing biological accuracy and reproducibility of analyses.
- **[Anders 2012]** Anders et al. (2012). Detecting differential usage of exons from RNA-seq data. *Genome Research*. [doi:10.1101/gr.133744.111](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.133744.111)  
  DEXSeq is an R/Bioconductor package that tests for differential exon usage in RNA-seq data using generalized linear models, controlling false discoveries by accounting for biological variation.
- **[Ching 2014]** Ching et al. (2014). Power analysis and sample size estimation for RNA-Seq differential expression. *RNA*. [doi:10.1261/rna.046011.114](https://doi.org/10.1261/rna.046011.114)  
  When designing RNA-Seq experiments, prioritize increasing biological sample size over sequencing depth to maximize statistical power for differential expression detection, especially beyond 20 million reads per sample.
- **[Risso 2014]** Risso et al. (2014). Normalization of RNA-seq data using factor analysis of control genes or samples. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/nbt.2931](https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt.2931)  
  RUV normalizes RNA-seq data by performing factor analysis on control genes (e.g., ERCC spike-ins) or control samples, improving fold-change estimation and differential expression testing over standard methods.
- **[Soneson 2016b]** Soneson et al. (2016). Isoform prefiltering improves performance of count-based methods for analysis of differential transcript usage. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-015-0862-3](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-015-0862-3)  
  Prefiltering the isoform annotation catalog before count-based differential transcript usage analysis (e.g., with DEXSeq) improves false discovery rate control, especially for genes with few isoforms.
- **[Muzellec 2023]** Muzellec et al. (2023). PyDESeq2: a python package for bulk RNA-seq differential expression analysis. *Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btad547](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btad547)  
  PyDESeq2 is an open-source Python re-implementation of the DESeq2 workflow for bulk RNA-seq differential expression analysis, achieving higher model likelihood and faster performance on large datasets like TCGA.

### Known and hidden factors

- **[Gagnon-Bartsch 2011]** Gagnon-Bartsch & Speed (2011). Using control genes to correct for unwanted variation in microarray data. *Biostatistics*. [doi:10.1093/biostatistics/kxr034](https://doi.org/10.1093/biostatistics/kxr034)  
  RUV-2, which restricts factor analysis to negative control genes to estimate unwanted variation, performs as well as or better than ComBat and SVA in gender-based differential expression studies of brain tissue, though extending it beyond differential expression analysis faces substantial unresolved challenges.
- **[Stegle 2012]** Stegle et al. (2012). Using probabilistic estimation of expression residuals (PEER) to obtain increased power and interpretability of gene expression analyses. *Nature Protocols*. [doi:10.1038/nprot.2011.457](https://doi.org/10.1038/nprot.2011.457)  
  PEER recommends inferring hidden factors from expression data (via factor analysis extending surrogate variable models) to use as additional covariates in eQTL mapping, increasing detection power, and separately analyzing these factors as phenotypes to interpret global expression variability causes; implementable within hours on a desktop computer.
- **[Gallego Romero 2014]** Gallego Romero et al. (2014). RNA-seq: impact of RNA degradation on transcript quantification. *BMC Biology*. [doi:10.1186/1741-7007-12-42](https://doi.org/10.1186/1741-7007-12-42)  
  RNA degradation affects transcript quantification non-uniformly across genes, so standard normalization alone fails to correct it; instead, including RNA Integrity Number (RIN) as a covariate in a linear model framework corrects most degradation-driven bias, recovering meaningful signal—provided RIN is not confounded with the biological effect of interest.
- **[Leek 2014]** Leek (2014). svaseq: removing batch effects and other unwanted noise from sequencing data. *Nucleic Acids Research*. [doi:10.1093/nar/gku864](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gku864)  
  The abstract contains no specific thresholds or numeric performance metrics; it only reports that svaseq (count/FPKM-adapted sva) and supervised ssva (using control probes) were compared against other batch-effect methods on simulated, count-based, and FPKM-based data, with implementation available in the sva Bioconductor package.
- **[Jaffe 2015]** Jaffe et al. (2015). Practical impacts of genomic data “cleaning” on biological discovery using surrogate variable analysis. *BMC Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1186/s12859-015-0808-5](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12859-015-0808-5)  
  SVA-based batch correction sharpens treatment-related differential expression (e.g., in stem cell differentiation) and preserves non-linear age effects in brain tissue, but can simultaneously erase biologically meaningful signal—sex, copy-number variation, and cell line/sample-specific effects—so guidelines should mandate careful a priori specification of biological variables of interest before applying SVA.
- **[Jaffe 2017]** Jaffe et al. (2017). qSVA framework for RNA quality correction in differential expression analysis. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*. [doi:10.1073/pnas.1617384114](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1617384114)  
  Existing RNA quality metrics fail to remove degradation-driven confounding when quality correlates with the outcome; qSVA—using degradation-associated expression regions as surrogate variables—should instead be applied, improving replication rates by more than 3× across independent postmortem brain RNA-seq studies of schizophrenia.
- **[Molania 2022]** Molania et al. (2022). Removing unwanted variation from large-scale RNA sequencing data with PRPS. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/s41587-022-01440-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-022-01440-w)  
  RUV-III with PRPS outperforms standard TCGA normalization for removing library size, tumor purity, and batch effects from RNA-seq data, improving cancer subtype identification, gene-survival association, and co-expression analyses; the method is also recommended for integrating/normalizing multi-lab or multi-platform transcriptomic datasets.
- **[Zhou 2022]** Zhou et al. (2022). PCA outperforms popular hidden variable inference methods for molecular QTL mapping. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-022-02761-4](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-022-02761-4)  
  PCA matched or outperformed SVA, PEER, and HCP for hidden-variable inference in molecular QTL mapping across 362 synthetic and 110 real datasets, while running orders of magnitude faster and being easier to interpret; guidelines should recommend PCA-based covariate adjustment (via the PCAForQTL package) over SVA/PEER/HCP for QTL analysis.

### Enrichment and activity inference

- **[Subramanian 2005]** Subramanian et al. (2005). Gene set enrichment analysis: A knowledge-based approach for interpreting genome-wide expression profiles. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*. [doi:10.1073/pnas.0506580102](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0506580102)  
  Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) identifies enriched biological pathways from genome-wide expression data by analyzing predefined gene sets rather than individual genes, revealing shared pathways missed by single-gene analysis across independent studies.
- **[Young 2010]** Young et al. (2010). Gene ontology analysis for RNA-seq: accounting for selection bias. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/gb-2010-11-2-r14](https://doi.org/10.1186/gb-2010-11-2-r14)  
  GOseq performs Gene Ontology analysis on RNA-seq data while correcting for length/expression-based selection bias in differential expression detection, yielding results more consistent with known biology than standard GO methods.
- **[Wu 2012]** Wu & Smyth (2012). Camera: a competitive gene set test accounting for inter-gene correlation. *Nucleic Acids Research*. [doi:10.1093/nar/gks461](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gks461)  
  CAMERA tests gene set enrichment while estimating and adjusting for inter-gene correlation, correctly controlling type I error where permutation-based competitive tests become severely inflated by such correlation.
- **[Tarca 2013]** Tarca et al. (2013). A Comparison of Gene Set Analysis Methods in Terms of Sensitivity, Prioritization and Specificity. *PLoS ONE*. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0079217](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0079217)  
  Across 42 real datasets and 16 gene set analysis methods, PLAGE, GLOBALTEST, and PADOG performed best overall with acceptably low false positive rates under phenotype permutation; MRGSE performed best among methods with higher-than-expected false positives; four methods showed large false positive rates, and prioritization performance diverged from sensitivity performance.
- **[Korotkevich 2016]** Korotkevich et al. (2016). Fast gene set enrichment analysis. *(preprint)* [doi:10.1101/060012](https://doi.org/10.1101/060012)  
  FGSEA rapidly and accurately estimates arbitrarily small GSEA P-values (down to 10⁻¹⁰⁰), enabling more sensitive pathway enrichment detection after multiple-hypothesis correction than standard implementations.
- **[Schubert 2018]** Schubert et al. (2018). Perturbation-response genes reveal signaling footprints in cancer gene expression. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/s41467-017-02391-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-02391-6)  
  PROGENy, built from a compendium of perturbation experiments, outperforms standard pathway-mapping approaches by recovering known driver mutation effects, providing stronger drug-indication markers, and correctly distinguishing oncogenic from tumor-suppressor pathways for patient survival—limitations gene-expression-to-pathway mapping fails to address due to ignored post-translational modifications.
- **[Garcia-Alonso 2019]** Garcia-Alonso et al. (2019). Benchmark and integration of resources for the estimation of human transcription factor activities. *Genome Research*. [doi:10.1101/gr.240663.118](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.240663.118)  
  Literature-curated TF–target interactions performed best for predicting TF activity, followed by ChIP-seq-derived regulons, while overlap between regulon strategies (manually curated, ChIP-seq, motif-based, and co-expression-derived) was low across 1541 human TFs—supporting use of the integrated, confidence-scored resource over any single source.
- **[Mandelboum 2019]** Mandelboum et al. (2019). Recurrent functional misinterpretation of RNA-seq data caused by sample-specific gene length bias. *PLOS Biology*. [doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.3000481](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000481)  
  Sample-specific gene length bias inflates GSEA false positives for short-gene (e.g., ribosomal protein) and long-gene (e.g., extracellular matrix) sets; RPKM, TMM, RLE, and quantile/upper-quartile normalization fail to correct it, whereas cqn and EDASeq (using gene length as a sample-specific covariate) effectively remove it, and correlation-aware gene-set tests reduce false positives at some cost to power.
- **[Reimand 2019]** Reimand et al. (2019). Pathway enrichment analysis and visualization of omics data using g:Profiler, GSEA, Cytoscape and EnrichmentMap. *Nature Protocols*. [doi:10.1038/s41596-018-0103-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41596-018-0103-9)  
  This protocol combines g:Profiler, GSEA, Cytoscape and EnrichmentMap into a step-by-step, ~4.5-hour workflow for deriving, statistically testing, and visualizing enriched pathways from omics-derived gene lists without prior bioinformatics training.
- **[Geistlinger 2020]** Geistlinger et al. (2020). Toward a gold standard for benchmarking gene set enrichment analysis. *Briefings in Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bib/bbz158](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbz158)  
  Benchmarking of 10 enrichment methods across 75 datasets (42 diseases) revealed significant differences in runtime, RNA-seq applicability, and recovery of relevance-ranked GO/KEGG terms; guidelines should cite practical recommendations for adapting microarray-designed methods to RNA-seq and selecting methods by gene-set-test type for optimal phenotype-relevant prioritization.
- **[Holland 2020]** Holland et al. (2020). Robustness and applicability of transcription factor and pathway analysis tools on single-cell RNA-seq data. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-020-1949-z](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-020-1949-z)  
  Bulk-based footprint tools (PROGENy, DoRothEA, GO enrichment) applied to scRNA-seq perform comparably to bulk data and partially outperform dedicated single-cell methods (SCENIC/AUCell, metaVIPER); tool performance depends more on the curated gene sets used than on the statistical method, and cell-type-specific variability is preserved across protocols.
- **[Badia-i-Mompel 2022]** Badia-i-Mompel et al. (2022). decoupleR: ensemble of computational methods to infer biological activities from omics data. *Bioinformatics Advances*. [doi:10.1093/bioadv/vbac016](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioadv/vbac016)  
  decoupleR integrates multiple methods and OmniPath prior knowledge to infer biological activities from omics data, finding that simple linear models and a consensus score across top methods best predict perturbed regulators.
- **[Fang 2022]** Fang et al. (2022). GSEApy: a comprehensive package for performing gene set enrichment analysis in Python. *Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btac757](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btac757)  
  GSEApy is a Python package that performs gene set enrichment analysis using a Rust-accelerated implementation, offering 3-fold faster speed and over 4-fold lower memory than its NumPy predecessor for large datasets.
- **[Müller-Dott 2023]** Müller-Dott et al. (2023). Expanding the coverage of regulons from high-confidence prior knowledge for accurate estimation of transcription factor activities. *Nucleic Acids Research*. [doi:10.1093/nar/gkad841](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkad841)  
  CollecTRI-derived signed regulons covering 1,186 TFs outperform other public TF-target resource collections (e.g., DoRothEA, TRRUST) for inferring TF activity changes from perturbation transcriptomics data, recommending CollecTRI as the preferred prior-knowledge resource for regulon-based TF activity estimation, including single-cell applications.

## Single-cell and single-nucleus RNA-seq (`SCRNASEQ_GUIDELINES.md`)

### Reviews, workflows and ecosystems

- **[Wolf 2018]** Wolf et al. (2018). SCANPY: large-scale single-cell gene expression data analysis. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-017-1382-0](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-017-1382-0)  
  SCANPY is a scalable Python toolkit—supporting preprocessing, visualization, clustering, pseudotime/trajectory inference, differential expression, and gene regulatory network simulation—that efficiently analyzes single-cell gene expression datasets exceeding one million cells, using the accompanying AnnData data structure.
- **[Amezquita 2019]** Amezquita et al. (2019). Orchestrating single-cell analysis with Bioconductor. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/s41592-019-0654-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-019-0654-x)  
  Bioconductor provides a standardized, community-developed ecosystem of R packages—with state-of-the-art methods, data infrastructure, and visualization tools—for analyzing single-cell genomic data, documented in the online book OSCA.
- **[Luecken 2019]** Luecken & Theis (2019). Current best practices in single‐cell RNA‐seq analysis: a tutorial. *Molecular Systems Biology*. [doi:10.15252/msb.20188746](https://doi.org/10.15252/msb.20188746)  
  This tutorial provides consensus best-practice recommendations—derived from independent comparison studies—for each step of single-cell RNA-seq preprocessing and downstream analysis, integrated into a reproducible workflow.
- **[Füllgrabe 2020]** Füllgrabe et al. (2020). Guidelines for reporting single-cell RNA-seq experiments. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/s41587-020-00744-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-020-00744-z)  
  minSCe: minimum information to report for single-cell RNA-seq experiments (samples, protocols, sequencing, processing).
- **[Gayoso 2022]** Gayoso et al. (2022). A Python library for probabilistic analysis of single-cell omics data. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/s41587-021-01206-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-021-01206-w)  
  scvi-tools: library of probabilistic models (scVI, scANVI, totalVI, …) for single-cell omics with a common API.
- **[Hao 2023]** Hao et al. (2023). Dictionary learning for integrative, multimodal and scalable single-cell analysis. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/s41587-023-01767-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-023-01767-y)  
  Seurat v5's bridge integration uses a multiomic dataset as a molecular dictionary to map single-cell data across modalities (RNA, ATAC, histone marks, methylation, protein) into a shared reference space.
- **[Heumos 2023]** Heumos et al. (2023). Best practices for single-cell analysis across modalities. *Nature Reviews Genetics*. [doi:10.1038/s41576-023-00586-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41576-023-00586-w)  
  This review synthesizes independent benchmarking studies across single-cell modalities (transcriptomics, chromatin accessibility, proteomics, immune repertoire, spatial) to recommend best-practice workflows for common unimodal and multimodal analysis steps.
- **[Virshup 2023]** Virshup et al. (2023). The scverse project provides a computational ecosystem for single-cell omics data analysis. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/s41587-023-01733-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-023-01733-8)  
  scverse: community ecosystem of interoperable Python packages built on AnnData/MuData (Scanpy, scvi-tools, Squidpy, …).

### Alignment and quantification

- **[Arzalluz-Luque 2018]** Arzalluz-Luque & Conesa (2018). Single-cell RNAseq for the study of isoforms—how is that possible?. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-018-1496-z](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-018-1496-z)  
  The paper establishes four necessary conditions for valid single-cell isoform-level analysis but provides no specific thresholds or winning method from the abstract alone; it concludes that whether isoform expression analysis is truly feasible at single-cell resolution remains an open question requiring these criteria be assessed.
- **[Ntranos 2019]** Ntranos et al. (2019). A discriminative learning approach to differential expression analysis for single-cell RNA-seq. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/s41592-018-0303-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-018-0303-9)  
  Using transcript compatibility counts from pseudoalignment enables quantification-free differential expression analysis of 3' single-cell RNA-seq, detecting both transcript dynamics and gene abundance changes, and identifying marker genes previously undetectable through standard quantification-based approaches.
- **[Lebrigand 2020]** Lebrigand et al. (2020). High throughput error corrected Nanopore single cell transcriptome sequencing. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/s41467-020-17800-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-17800-6)  
  ScNaUmi-seq combines Nanopore long-read sequencing with accurate 10x Genomics cell barcode/UMI assignment and UMI-guided error correction, enabling high-depth, full-length transcript sequencing at single-cell resolution—recommended for resolving splicing isoforms and SNVs (e.g., RNA editing) that short-read 3'/5'-end methods and prior low-depth long-read approaches miss.
- **[Kaminow 2021]** Kaminow et al. (2021). STARsolo: accurate, fast and versatile mapping/quantification of single-cell and single-nucleus RNA-seq data. *(preprint)* [doi:10.1101/2021.05.05.442755](https://doi.org/10.1101/2021.05.05.442755)  
  STARsolo matches CellRanger's quantification results while running significantly faster, and outperforms pseudoalignment-to-transcriptome tools in accuracy on realistic simulated scRNA-seq data; guidelines should prefer STARsolo over pseudoalignment methods and note its ability to properly handle multi-gene reads for detecting certain biologically important gene classes.
- **[Melsted 2021]** Melsted et al. (2021). Modular, efficient and constant-memory single-cell RNA-seq preprocessing. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/s41587-021-00870-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-021-00870-2)  
  The kallisto|bustools workflow offers near-optimal speed with constant memory usage, enabling scalable scRNA-seq preprocessing regardless of dataset size, and its modular design supports flexible downstream applications including RNA velocity analysis.
- **[Soneson 2021]** Soneson et al. (2021). Preprocessing choices affect RNA velocity results for droplet scRNA-seq data. *PLOS Computational Biology*. [doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008585](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008585)  
  Thirteen quantification approaches across five tools (five datasets) showed substantial discrepancies in spliced/unspliced RNA abundance estimates, which propagated downstream to alter inferred RNA velocities and biological interpretation—indicating genomic feature definition and quantification algorithm choice both require careful, deliberate consideration in velocity workflows.
- **[Brüning 2022]** Brüning et al. (2022). Comparative analysis of common alignment tools for single-cell RNA sequencing. *GigaScience*. [doi:10.1093/gigascience/giac001](https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/giac001)  
  Kallisto reported the most cells but inflated low-content, unknown-cell-type populations and spuriously detected Vmn/Olfr gene-family "mapping artefacts"; Alevin rarely produced such low-quality cells. STARsolo, Cell Ranger 6, Alevin-fry, and Alevin yielded similar, more reliable gene sets, and mitochondrial content varied with prefiltered versus full annotation sets.
- **[He 2022]** He et al. (2022). Alevin-fry unlocks rapid, accurate and memory-frugal quantification of single-cell RNA-seq data. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/s41592-022-01408-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-022-01408-3)  
  Alevin-fry outperforms other accurate quantification tools in speed and memory efficiency, while resolving the memory-scalability and false-positive expression issues seen in lightweight alternatives. It also enables extraction of spliced/unspliced counts for RNA velocity directly from standard preprocessing output, without separate pipelines.
- **[Sullivan 2024]** Sullivan et al. (2024). Accurate quantification of nascent and mature RNAs from single-cell and single-nucleus RNA-seq. *Nucleic Acids Research*. [doi:10.1093/nar/gkae1137](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkae1137)  
  Expanding the mapping reference to include both nascent and mature mRNA sequences, combined with identifying distinguishing flanking k-mers (DFKs) as a background filter, reduces mismapping of intronic/pre-mRNA reads into mature transcript counts and improves accurate assignment of ambiguous reads in single-cell/single-nucleus RNA-seq quantification.

### Quality control: cells, droplets, ambient RNA, doublets, artefacts

- **[Lun 2019]** Lun et al. (2019). EmptyDrops: distinguishing cells from empty droplets in droplet-based single-cell RNA sequencing data. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-019-1662-y](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-019-1662-y)  
  EmptyDrops identifies real cells in droplet-based scRNA-seq by testing for significant deviations from the ambient RNA profile, retaining distinct cell types that conventional knee-point methods discard.
- **[Wolock 2019]** Wolock et al. (2019). Scrublet: Computational Identification of Cell Doublets in Single-Cell Transcriptomic Data. *Cell Systems*. [doi:10.1016/j.cels.2018.11.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2018.11.005)  
  Scrublet identifies cell doublets in single-cell RNA-seq data by simulating synthetic multiplets from the dataset and building a nearest-neighbor classifier, without requiring expert knowledge or cell clustering.
- **[Bernstein 2020]** Bernstein et al. (2020). Solo: Doublet Identification in Single-Cell RNA-Seq via Semi-Supervised Deep Learning. *Cell Systems*. [doi:10.1016/j.cels.2020.05.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2020.05.010)  
  Solo, a semi-supervised VAE-based classifier trained on simulated doublets, outperforms existing computational doublet-detection methods in accuracy; it is recommended as a complementary step after experimental doublet-removal techniques to further purify scRNA-seq datasets of residual doublets before downstream analysis.
- **[Osorio 2020]** Osorio & Cai (2020). Systematic determination of the mitochondrial proportion in human and mice tissues for single-cell RNA-sequencing data quality control. *Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa751](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa751)  
  The default 5% mitochondrial threshold is often unsuitable for human scRNA-seq data, so tissue-specific mtDNA% reference values (121 mouse, 44 human tissues) should guide quality control instead.
- **[Yang 2020]** Yang et al. (2020). Decontamination of ambient RNA in single-cell RNA-seq with DecontX. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-020-1950-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-020-1950-6)  
  DecontX models the counts of each cell as a mixture of its native expression and contamination from the other cell populations, estimates the contamination with a Bayesian model and removes it.
- **[Young 2020]** Young & Behjati (2020). SoupX removes ambient RNA contamination from droplet-based single-cell RNA sequencing data. *GigaScience*. [doi:10.1093/gigascience/giaa151](https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/giaa151)  
  SoupX estimates and removes ambient cell-free RNA contamination ("soup") from droplet-based single-cell RNA-seq data, yielding background-corrected expression profiles compatible with standard downstream analysis tools.
- **[Germain 2021]** Germain et al. (2021). Doublet identification in single-cell sequencing data using scDblFinder. *F1000Research*. [doi:10.12688/f1000research.73600.1](https://doi.org/10.12688/f1000research.73600.1)  
  scDblFinder: fast doublet detection combining simulated doublets and a classifier; detects most heterotypic doublets and ranked first in an independent benchmark.
- **[Heiser 2021]** Heiser et al. (2021). Automated quality control and cell identification of droplet-based single-cell data using dropkick. *Genome Research*. [doi:10.1101/gr.271908.120](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.271908.120)  
  Dropkick, a weakly supervised model trained on automatically generated heuristic labels, outperformed conventional fixed-count thresholding and EmptyDrops for scRNA-seq quality control, recovering more rare cell types while better excluding ambient/empty droplets, with improved robustness to dataset-specific variation in both low- and high-ambient-background datasets.
- **[Hippen 2021]** Hippen et al. (2021). miQC: An adaptive probabilistic framework for quality control of single-cell RNA-sequencing data. *PLOS Computational Biology*. [doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009290](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009290)  
  miQC jointly models mitochondrial read proportion and detected gene count using mixture models to adaptively identify low-quality scRNA-seq cells, avoiding arbitrary uniform thresholds across diverse tissue types.
- **[Muskovic 2021]** Muskovic & Powell (2021). DropletQC: improved identification of empty droplets and damaged cells in single-cell RNA-seq data. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-021-02547-0](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-021-02547-0)  
  DropletQC identifies empty droplets and damaged cells in droplet-based scRNA-seq data using nuclear fraction, the proportion of unspliced pre-mRNA, and integrates with methods like EmptyDrops for improved quality control.
- **[Xi 2021]** Xi & Li (2021). Benchmarking Computational Doublet-Detection Methods for Single-Cell RNA Sequencing Data. *Cell Systems*. [doi:10.1016/j.cels.2020.11.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2020.11.008)  
  For doublet detection in scRNA-seq data, DoubletFinder offers the best detection accuracy while cxds provides the highest computational efficiency, per benchmarking across 16 real and 112 synthetic datasets.
- **[Marsh 2022]** Marsh et al. (2022). Dissection of artifactual and confounding glial signatures by single-cell sequencing of mouse and human brain. *Nature Neuroscience*. [doi:10.1038/s41593-022-01022-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-022-01022-8)  
  Use a validated dissociation protocol minimizing enzymatic processing time/temperature for brain tissue, since standard enzymatic dissociation induces artifactual ex vivo microglial gene expression signatures confounding single-cell/nucleus RNA-seq analyses.
- **[Xiong 2022]** Xiong et al. (2022). Chord: an ensemble machine learning algorithm to identify doublets in single-cell RNA sequencing data. *Communications Biology*. [doi:10.1038/s42003-022-03476-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-022-03476-9)  
  Chord, an ensemble method integrating multiple doublet-detection tools via machine learning, outperformed individual single-method approaches (e.g., higher accuracy/stability) across real and synthetic scRNA-seq datasets, addressing the instability of standalone methods; its modular design also allows incorporation of new detection tools.
- **[Fleming 2023]** Fleming et al. (2023). Unsupervised removal of systematic background noise from droplet-based single-cell experiments using CellBender. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/s41592-023-01943-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-023-01943-7)  
  CellBender is a deep generative model that removes systematic background noise from droplet-based scRNA-seq/snRNA-seq/CITE-seq data by distinguishing cell-containing from cell-free droplets and providing end-to-end noise-free quantification.
- **[Pool 2023]** Pool et al. (2023). Recovery of missing single-cell RNA-sequencing data with optimized transcriptomic references. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/s41592-023-02003-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-023-02003-w)  
  Optimizing transcriptomic references—by recovering false intergenic reads, using hybrid pre-mRNA mapping, and resolving gene overlaps—recovers missing scRNA-seq gene expression, improving cellular resolution and warranting reanalysis of existing datasets.
- **[Montserrat-Ayuso 2024]** Montserrat-Ayuso & Esteve-Codina (2024). High content of nuclei-free low-quality cells in reference single-cell atlases: a call for more stringent quality control using nuclear fraction. *BMC Genomics*. [doi:10.1186/s12864-024-11015-5](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-024-11015-5)  
  Single-cell RNA-seq quality control should incorporate cell intronic fraction (or MALAT1 expression as a proxy) to detect and filter nuclei-free, low-quality cells overlooked by current permissive standards.
- **[Meier 2026]** Meier et al. (2026). Order Matters: Ambient RNA correction confounds doublet detection in single-cell RNA sequencing. *(preprint)* [doi:10.64898/2026.10.13.758369](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.13.758369)  
  On 10x Flex data with engineered doublets and on three multiplexed datasets (cells and nuclei), six doublet detectors found as many or more true doublets on uncorrected counts as after SoupX or decontX correction; Scrublet nearly failed after decontX. Both tools removed more counts from the doublets they hid, while CellBender, which models doublets, mostly matched uncorrected counts. Recommends doublet detection before SoupX or decontX, and after CellBender.
- **[Gayoso 2020]** Gayoso, Shor, Carr, Sharma & Pe'er (2020). DoubletDetection (software, version 3.0). *Zenodo*. [doi:10.5281/zenodo.2678041](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.2678041)  
  Doublet classifier that repeatedly simulates doublets, clusters observed and synthetic cells together, and scores cells by hypergeometric enrichment of synthetic doublets in their cluster across iterations. Software record; metadata taken from the package README (not in Crossref).

### Normalization, features and visualization

- **[Chung 2014]** Chung & Storey (2014). Statistical significance of variables driving systematic variation in high-dimensional data. *Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btu674](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btu674)  
  The jackstraw method accurately estimates statistical significance of gene associations with PCA-derived latent variables, avoiding inflated significance from over-fitting inherent in conventional methods. Applied to yeast cell-cycle data, it identified cell-cycle-regulated genes; on post-trauma expression data, it showed greater enrichment for inflammatory gene sets than clinically defined phenotypes.
- **[Gavish 2014]** Gavish & Donoho (2014). The Optimal Hard Threshold for Singular Values is \(4/\sqrt {3}\). *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory*. [doi:10.1109/tit.2014.2323359](https://doi.org/10.1109/tit.2014.2323359)  
  Optimal hard threshold for singular values of a noisy low-rank matrix: an objective criterion for the number of components when noise is homogeneous.
- **[Lun 2016]** Lun et al. (2016). Pooling across cells to normalize single-cell RNA sequencing data with many zero counts. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-016-0947-7](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-016-0947-7)  
  Pool-based deconvolution of size factors across groups of cells outperforms existing methods for normalizing single-cell RNA-seq data dominated by zero counts, improving downstream analysis accuracy.
- **[Fortmann 2023]** Fortmann (2023). scranPY: a Python implementation of r-scran::computeSumFactors, normalization by deconvolution for single-cell RNA-sequencing (software, version 0.1). *Zenodo*. [doi:10.5281/zenodo.10072109](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.10072109)  
  Python port of the pooling-and-deconvolution size factors of scran (Lun 2016); requires a dense count matrix. Software record; metadata taken from the repository README (not in Crossref).
- **[Vallejos 2017]** Vallejos et al. (2017). Normalizing single-cell RNA sequencing data: challenges and opportunities. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/nmeth.4292](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.4292)  
  Commonly used bulk RNA-seq/microarray normalization methods can produce misleading results when applied to single-cell RNA-seq data; the paper recommends alternative normalization approaches specifically suited to single-cell transcriptomics rather than defaulting to standard bulk methods.
- **[Hafemeister 2019]** Hafemeister & Satija (2019). Normalization and variance stabilization of single-cell RNA-seq data using regularized negative binomial regression. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-019-1874-1](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-019-1874-1)  
  sctransform uses regularized negative binomial regression on UMI counts, with sequencing depth as a covariate and parameter pooling across similar-abundance genes, to normalize scRNA-seq data without pseudocounts or log-transformation.
- **[Kobak 2019]** Kobak & Berens (2019). The art of using t-SNE for single-cell transcriptomics. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/s41467-019-13056-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-13056-x)  
  Using PCA initialisation, a high learning rate, and multi-scale similarity kernels (plus exaggeration and downsampling for large datasets) yields more faithful t-SNE visualisations of single-cell transcriptomic data.
- **[Townes 2019]** Townes et al. (2019). Feature selection and dimension reduction for single-cell RNA-Seq based on a multinomial model. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-019-1861-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-019-1861-6)  
  For scRNA-Seq with UMI counts, use multinomial-based methods—GLM-PCA for dimension reduction and deviance-based feature selection—instead of log-CPM normalization and highly variable gene selection, which introduce false variability.
- **[Lause 2021]** Lause et al. (2021). Analytic Pearson residuals for normalization of single-cell RNA-seq UMI data. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-021-02451-7](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-021-02451-7)  
  Analytic Pearson residuals (a parsimonious, closed-form reformulation of Hafemeister/Satija's model, equivalent to rank-one Poisson GLM-PCA) outperform negative-binomial regression residuals, variance-stabilizing transformations, and GLM-PCA for selecting biologically variable genes and for dimensionality reduction; UMI technical noise is close to Poisson, with overdispersion independent of gene expression rather than gene-specific.
- **[Zappia 2025]** Zappia et al. (2025). Feature selection methods affect the performance of scRNA-seq data integration and querying. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/s41592-025-02624-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-025-02624-3)  
  For scRNA-seq integration and reference mapping, select highly variable features (common practice is effective), and consider batch-aware or lineage-specific selection depending on integration method and biological question.

### Integration and batch correction

- **[Lopez 2018]** Lopez et al. (2018). Deep generative modeling for single-cell transcriptomics. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/s41592-018-0229-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-018-0229-2)  
  scVI uses deep neural networks and stochastic optimization to probabilistically model single-cell gene expression, accounting for batch effects and limited sensitivity, enabling accurate batch correction, visualization, clustering, and differential expression.
- **[Korsunsky 2019]** Korsunsky et al. (2019). Fast, sensitive and accurate integration of single-cell data with Harmony. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/s41592-019-0619-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-019-0619-0)  
  Harmony is an algorithm that integrates single-cell RNA-seq data across datasets/technologies into a shared embedding by cell type, outperforming prior methods while using fewer computational resources.
- **[Tran 2020]** Tran et al. (2020). A benchmark of batch-effect correction methods for single-cell RNA sequencing data. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-019-1850-9](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-019-1850-9)  
  Harmony, LIGER, and Seurat 3 are the best-performing batch-effect correction methods for scRNA-seq integration, with Harmony recommended first due to its substantially shorter runtime.
- **[Lotfollahi 2021]** Lotfollahi et al. (2021). Mapping single-cell data to reference atlases by transfer learning. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/s41587-021-01001-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-021-01001-7)  
  scArches maps new single-cell query datasets onto existing reference atlases via transfer learning, removing batch effects and preserving biological and disease state information while using far fewer parameters than de novo integration.
- **[Luecken 2021]** Luecken et al. (2021). Benchmarking atlas-level data integration in single-cell genomics. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/s41592-021-01336-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-021-01336-8)  
  For atlas-level single-cell integration, scANVI, Scanorama, scVI and scGen perform best overall, especially on complex tasks, while highly variable gene selection—not scaling—improves integration performance.
- **[Antonsson 2025]** Antonsson & Melsted (2025). Batch correction methods used in single-cell RNA sequencing analyses are often poorly calibrated. *Genome Research*. [doi:10.1101/gr.279886.124](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.279886.124)  
  Among eight widely used scRNA-seq batch correction methods tested, Harmony consistently avoided introducing detectable artifacts and is the only method recommended for batch correction of single-cell RNA sequencing data.

- **[Tang 2020]** Tang et al. (2020). Evaluating single-cell cluster stability using the Jaccard similarity index. *Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa956](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa956)

- **[Clarke 2021]** Clarke et al. (2021). Tutorial: guidelines for annotating single-cell transcriptomic maps using automated and manual methods. *Nature Protocols*. [doi:10.1038/s41596-021-00534-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41596-021-00534-0)

- **[Pasquini 2021]** Pasquini et al. (2021). Automated methods for cell type annotation on scRNA-seq data. *Computational and Structural Biotechnology Journal*. [doi:10.1016/j.csbj.2021.01.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csbj.2021.01.015)

- **[Domínguez Conde 2022]** Domínguez Conde et al. (2022). Cross-tissue immune cell analysis reveals tissue-specific features in humans. *Science*. [doi:10.1126/science.abl5197](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abl5197)  
  CellTypist is a machine learning tool that enables rapid, precise cross-tissue immune cell type annotation from single-cell RNA sequencing data, leveraging a common reference dataset.

  ScType provides fully-automated, ultra-fast cell-type annotation from scRNA-seq data by enforcing specificity of positive and negative marker genes across clusters, validated on six human/mouse tissue datasets, and can further distinguish malignant from healthy cells via single-cell SNV calling.
- **[Yu 2022]** Yu et al. (2022). Benchmarking clustering algorithms on estimating the number of cell types from single-cell RNA-sequencing data. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-022-02622-0](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-022-02622-0)  
  The abstract reports no specific numeric thresholds, but recommends the stability-based scCCESS approach (R package) for estimating cell-type numbers, based on benchmarking deviation from true count, estimation variability, clustering concordance, runtime, and peak memory across varied cell-type numbers, sizes, and proportions in Tabula Muris/Sapiens datasets.
- **[Grabski 2023]** Grabski et al. (2023). Significance analysis for clustering with single-cell RNA-sequencing data. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/s41592-023-01933-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-023-01933-9)  
  Without formal significance testing, standard scRNA-seq clustering workflows show overconfidence and over-clustering, falsely inflating novel cell-type discovery. A model-based hypothesis-testing extension of significance-of-hierarchical-clustering, adaptable to any algorithm's output and batch structure, outperformed popular clustering workflows when benchmarked on Human Lung Cell Atlas and mouse cerebellar cortex data.

  scCODA is a Bayesian model for compositional single-cell data analysis that reliably detects cell type composition changes while controlling false discoveries, outperforming conventional methods on low sample sizes.
- **[Dann 2021]** Dann et al. (2021). Differential abundance testing on single-cell data using k-nearest neighbor graphs. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/s41587-021-01033-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-021-01033-z)  
  Milo performs differential abundance testing on k-nearest neighbor graph neighborhoods rather than discrete clusters, enabling detection of perturbations and continuous trajectory changes that cluster-based methods obscure.
- **[Zhao 2021b]** Zhao et al. (2021). Detection of differentially abundant cell subpopulations in scRNA-seq data. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*. [doi:10.1073/pnas.2100293118](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2100293118)  
  DA-seq, a multiscale k-nearest-neighbor method, detects differentially abundant cell subpopulations without relying on predefined clustering, outperforming cluster-based approaches that miss DA populations misaligned with cluster boundaries. It recovered previously identified DA cell types in COVID-19 severity, melanoma immunotherapy response, embryonic development, and brain aging datasets while revealing additional, previously undescribed DA subpopulations.
- **[Phipson 2022]** Phipson et al. (2022). propeller: testing for differences in cell type proportions in single cell data. *Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btac582](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btac582)  
  Propeller, implemented in the speckle R package, robustly tests for statistically significant differences in cell type proportions between experimental conditions using scRNA-seq data with biological replication.
- **[Mangiola 2023]** Mangiola et al. (2023). sccomp: Robust differential composition and variability analysis for single-cell data. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*. [doi:10.1073/pnas.2203828120](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2203828120)  
  sccomp outperforms existing differential composition methods by jointly modeling count distribution, compositionality, group-specific variability, and proportion mean-variability association while accounting for outliers; guidelines should note that Dirichlet-multinomial models are inadequate since mean-variability association is ubiquitous across single-cell technologies.
- **[Yi 2024]** Yi et al. (2024). Benchmarking differential abundance methods for finding condition-specific prototypical cells in multi-sample single-cell datasets. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-023-03143-0](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-023-03143-0)  
  Benchmark of six differential abundance methods: no method best overall; Meld most accurate without batch effects, Milo best with them; including batch in the model improves all; all fail under strong cell-type imbalance.

### Cell-cell communication

- **[Armingol 2020]** Armingol et al. (2020). Deciphering cell–cell interactions and communication from gene expression. *Nature Reviews Genetics*. [doi:10.1038/s41576-020-00292-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41576-020-00292-x)  
  This Review synthesizes ligand-receptor inference methods from bulk and single-cell RNA-seq, highlighting that tools leveraging curated protein-protein/ligand-receptor interaction databases combined with coordinated gene expression enable intercellular communication discovery, without specifying particular performance thresholds or a single best-performing method from the abstract alone.
- **[Dimitrov 2022]** Dimitrov et al. (2022). Comparison of methods and resources for cell-cell communication inference from single-cell RNA-Seq data. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/s41467-022-30755-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-30755-0)  
  Both the choice of ligand-receptor resource and inference method substantially affect predicted cell-cell communication events, so LIANA offers a unified framework integrating 16 resources and 7 methods for robust analysis.
- **[Liu 2022]** Liu et al. (2022). Evaluation of cell-cell interaction methods by integrating single-cell RNA sequencing data with spatial information. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-022-02783-y](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-022-02783-y)  
  CellChat, CellPhoneDB, NicheNet, and ICELLNET outperformed other tools (statistical-based methods beat network-based and ST-based methods) when benchmarked across 16 tools on 15 simulated and 5 real datasets using a distance enrichment score; predictions were highly tool-dependent, so results from at least two methods should be combined to ensure accuracy.
- **[Dimitrov 2024]** Dimitrov et al. (2024). LIANA+ provides an all-in-one framework for cell–cell communication inference. *Nature Cell Biology*. [doi:10.1038/s41556-024-01469-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-024-01469-w)  
  The abstract provides no specific thresholds, performance comparisons, or failure modes—only that LIANA+ unifies existing methods into a scalable framework for inferring cell-cell communication across single-cell and spatial, single- and multi-condition, and multi-omics datasets.

### Trajectories, fate mapping and RNA velocity

- **[Haghverdi 2016]** Haghverdi et al. (2016). Diffusion pseudotime robustly reconstructs lineage branching. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/nmeth.3971](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.3971)  
  Diffusion pseudotime (DPT) uses diffusion-like random walks on single-cell expression data to robustly order cells along differentiation trajectories, enabling identification of transient/metastable states, branch points, and differentiation endpoints without requiring prior assumptions about trajectory structure.
- **[La Manno 2018]** La Manno et al. (2018). RNA velocity of single cells. *Nature*. [doi:10.1038/s41586-018-0414-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0414-6)  
  RNA velocity, estimated from the ratio of unspliced to spliced mRNA reads in standard scRNA-seq data, predicts individual cells' future expression states on a timescale of hours, validated in neural crest lineage data and applied to reveal branching lineage trees in developing mouse hippocampus and human embryonic brain.
- **[Street 2018]** Street et al. (2018). Slingshot: cell lineage and pseudotime inference for single-cell transcriptomics. *BMC Genomics*. [doi:10.1186/s12864-018-4772-0](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-018-4772-0)  
  Slingshot outperformed competing lineage-inference methods in simulation studies, producing more accurate pseudotime estimates, and correctly recovered biological signal across datasets with one to three branching trajectories, demonstrating robustness to noise in single-cell transcriptomic data.
- **[Saelens 2019]** Saelens et al. (2019). A comparison of single-cell trajectory inference methods. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/s41587-019-0071-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-019-0071-9)  
  Method choice for single-cell trajectory inference should be guided primarily by dataset dimensions and expected trajectory topology, per benchmarking of 45 methods across 110 real and 229 synthetic datasets.
- **[Setty 2019]** Setty et al. (2019). Characterization of cell fate probabilities in single-cell data with Palantir. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/s41587-019-0068-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-019-0068-4)  
  Palantir outperforms existing trajectory-detection algorithms in identifying cell lineages and recapitulating gene expression trends during differentiation, using entropy-based pseudo-time ordering and per-cell terminal-state fate probabilities, validated on human bone marrow hematopoietic differentiation and generalizable across diverse tissue types.
- **[Wolf 2019]** Wolf et al. (2019). PAGA: graph abstraction reconciles clustering with trajectory inference through a topology preserving map of single cells. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-019-1663-x](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-019-1663-x)  
  PAGA preserves global topology of single-cell data across resolutions and reconciles clustering with trajectory inference; validated on four hematopoietic datasets, adult planaria, and zebrafish embryo, with benchmarked computational efficiency scaling to one million neurons.
- **[Bergen 2020]** Bergen et al. (2020). Generalizing RNA velocity to transient cell states through dynamical modeling. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/s41587-020-0591-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-020-0591-3)  
  scVelo solves full transcriptional splicing dynamics via a likelihood-based model, generalizing RNA velocity to transient cell states beyond steady-state assumptions, enabling kinetic rate inference and driver gene detection.
- **[Van den Berge 2020]** Van den Berge et al. (2020). Trajectory-based differential expression analysis for single-cell sequencing data. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/s41467-020-14766-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-14766-3)  
  tradeSeq uses negative binomial generalized additive models with observation-level weights to flexibly detect within-lineage and between-lineage differential expression along single-cell trajectories, while accommodating zero inflation.
- **[Bergen 2021]** Bergen et al. (2021). RNA velocity—current challenges and future perspectives. *Molecular Systems Biology*. [doi:10.15252/msb.202110282](https://doi.org/10.15252/msb.202110282)  
  This review identifies limitations and pitfalls of current RNA velocity modeling approaches (no single "best" method specified) and recommends practices for addressing these failure modes, while outlining extensions to broader biological systems and additional omics modalities.
- **[Gorin 2022]** Gorin et al. (2022). RNA velocity unraveled. *PLOS Computational Biology*. [doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010492](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010492)  
  RNA velocity analyses should move beyond standard steady-state/dynamical assumptions and adopt a rigorous Markovian framework to improve robustness and mitigate known sensitivity to parameter choices and biological violations.
- **[Lange 2022]** Lange et al. (2022). CellRank for directed single-cell fate mapping. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/s41592-021-01346-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-021-01346-6)  
  CellRank integrates trajectory inference with RNA velocity directionality to identify initial, intermediate, and terminal cell states and compute fate probabilities without requiring known process direction; it correctly recovers lineage-traced reprogramming outcomes and identifies a novel, experimentally confirmed dedifferentiation trajectory with intermediate states during lung regeneration.
- **[Gayoso 2023]** Gayoso et al. (2023). Deep generative modeling of transcriptional dynamics for RNA velocity analysis in single cells. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/s41592-023-01994-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-023-01994-w)  
  veloVI outperformed existing RNA velocity methods (e.g., scVelo) on goodness of fit, cross-cell consistency, and stability across preprocessing pipelines, while providing transcriptome-wide posterior uncertainty estimates that can flag datasets where velocity analysis is inappropriate, and supports extension to time-dependent transcription rate models.
- **[Hou 2023]** Hou et al. (2023). A statistical framework for differential pseudotime analysis with multiple single-cell RNA-seq samples. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/s41467-023-42841-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-42841-y)  
  Lamian is a statistical framework for multi-sample pseudotime analysis that accounts for cross-sample variability, reducing sample-specific false discoveries when comparing trajectories, gene expression, and cell density across conditions.
- **[Zheng 2023]** Zheng et al. (2023). Pumping the brakes on RNA velocity by understanding and interpreting RNA velocity estimates. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-023-03065-x](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-023-03065-x)  
  RNA velocity accuracy depends heavily on k-NN graph quality; when the k-NN graph misrepresents true data structure, both direction and speed estimates show substantial errors. Speed estimation is unreliable except under very low noise; the authors introduce a quality measure to flag when velocity should not be trusted, and warn against using velocity to validate embeddings (circular reasoning).
- **[Kang 2024]** Kang et al. (2024). Mapping single-cell developmental potential in health and disease with interpretable deep learning. *(preprint)* [doi:10.1101/2024.03.19.585637](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.03.19.585637)  
  CytoTRACE 2, an interpretable deep learning framework, outperformed existing methods for recovering experimentally determined potency levels and differentiation states across 31 human/mouse scRNA-seq datasets (28 tissue types), accurately reconstructing mouse embryogenesis's temporal hierarchy across 62 timepoints and identifying potency-linked expression programs relevant to cancer prognosis and immunotherapy resistance.
- **[Weiler 2024]** Weiler et al. (2024). CellRank 2: unified fate mapping in multiview single-cell data. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/s41592-024-02303-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-024-02303-9)  
  CellRank 2 unifies trajectory inference across data modalities (expression similarity, RNA velocity, time-series, metabolic labeling) at million-cell scale, consistently recovering terminal states and fate probabilities in human hematopoiesis and endoderm development, and identifying genes driving medullary thymic epithelial cell formation during pharyngeal endoderm development.

### Cell segmentation

- **[Stringer 2020]** Stringer et al. (2020). Cellpose: a generalist algorithm for cellular segmentation. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/s41592-020-01018-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-020-01018-x)

- **[Greenwald 2021]** Greenwald et al. (2021). Whole-cell segmentation of tissue images with human-level performance using large-scale data annotation and deep learning. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/s41587-021-01094-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-021-01094-0)

- **[Petukhov 2021]** Petukhov et al. (2021). Cell segmentation in imaging-based spatial transcriptomics. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/s41587-021-01044-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-021-01044-w)

- **[Jones 2025]** Jones et al. (2025). Cell simulation as cell segmentation. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/s41592-025-02697-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-025-02697-0)  
  Proseg, a probabilistic ab initio cell simulation method, infers more accurate, computationally efficient cell boundaries in spatial transcriptomics, improving detection of tumor-infiltrating immune cells like neutrophils and T cells.

### Deconvolution

- **[Cable 2021]** Cable et al. (2021). Robust decomposition of cell type mixtures in spatial transcriptomics. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/s41587-021-00830-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-021-00830-w)

- **[Kleshchevnikov 2022]** Kleshchevnikov et al. (2022). Cell2location maps fine-grained cell types in spatial transcriptomics. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/s41587-021-01139-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-021-01139-4)

- **[Li 2022b]** Li et al. (2022). Benchmarking spatial and single-cell transcriptomics integration methods for transcript distribution prediction and cell type deconvolution. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/s41592-022-01480-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-022-01480-9)

- **[Sang-aram 2024]** Sang-aram et al. (2024). Spotless, a reproducible pipeline for benchmarking cell type deconvolution in spatial transcriptomics. *eLife*. [doi:10.7554/elife.88431.3](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.88431.3)

- **[Dong 2022]** Dong & Zhang (2022). Deciphering spatial domains from spatially resolved transcriptomics with an adaptive graph attention auto-encoder. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/s41467-022-29439-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-29439-6)

### Statistical pitfalls

- **[Bhuva 2024]** Bhuva et al. (2024). Library size confounds biology in spatial transcriptomics data. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-024-03241-7](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-024-03241-7)

## Sex inference (`SEX_INFERENCE_GUIDELINES.md`)

### Markers, references, loss of Y and genotype-based sex

- **[Wijchers 2010]** Wijchers et al. (2010). Sexual Dimorphism in Mammalian Autosomal Gene Regulation Is Determined Not Only by Sry but by Sex Chromosome Complement As Well. *Developmental Cell*. [doi:10.1016/j.devcel.2010.08.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2010.08.005)  
  Using heterochromatin-sensitive reporter silencing and genome-wide transcription profiling in sex-reversal mouse models, the study found X chromosome complement—not gonadal sex—drives differential gene silencing, with hundreds of autosomal genes sensitive to sex chromosome complement, and Sry modulating autosomal expression in a sex-chromosome-complement-specific manner.
- **[Staedtler 2013]** Staedtler et al. (2013). Robust and tissue-independent gender-specific transcript biomarkers. *Biomarkers*. [doi:10.3109/1354750x.2013.811538](https://doi.org/10.3109/1354750x.2013.811538)  
  A five-gene panel (RPS4Y1, EIF1AY, DDX3Y, KDM5D, XIST), identified from microarray expression in 123 blood samples (58F/65M), accurately predicted donor gender across 13 healthy and 11 cancerous tissue collections and external datasets, with concordant results by RT-PCR, supporting its use as a robust, tissue- and platform-independent gender determination method.
- **[Buckberry 2014]** Buckberry et al. (2014). massiR : a method for predicting the sex of samples in gene expression microarray datasets. *Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btu161](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btu161)  
  massiR predicts sample sex in microarray datasets lacking metadata by applying unsupervised clustering (e.g., k-means/hierarchical) to Y-chromosome probe expression values, classifying samples into male/female groups—useful for confirming sex annotations or enabling sex-stratified meta-analyses in mammalian expression datasets. No specific accuracy thresholds are given in the abstract.
- **[Forsberg 2014]** Forsberg et al. (2014). Mosaic loss of chromosome Y in peripheral blood is associated with shorter survival and higher risk of cancer. *Nature Genetics*. [doi:10.1038/ng.2966](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.2966)  
  Mosaic loss of chromosome Y (LOY) in peripheral blood, detected in ≥8.2–20.5% of elderly men across two cohorts, was associated with increased all-cause mortality (HR=1.91–3.66) and non-hematological cancer mortality (HR=3.62), with LOY carriers showing 5.5 years shorter median survival, supporting LOY as a candidate predictive biomarker for male carcinogenesis.
- **[Toker 2016]** Toker et al. (2016). Whose sample is it anyway? Widespread misannotation of samples in transcriptomics studies. *F1000Research*. [doi:10.12688/f1000research.9471.1](https://doi.org/10.12688/f1000research.9471.1)  
  Comparing reported sex with sex-specific gene expression, 46% of public human transcriptomics datasets contained apparently mislabelled samples (99% lower bound 33%).
- **[Pedersen 2017]** Pedersen & Quinlan (2017). Who’s Who? Detecting and Resolving Sample Anomalies in Human DNA Sequencing Studies with Peddy. *The American Journal of Human Genetics*. [doi:10.1016/j.ajhg.2017.01.017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajhg.2017.01.017)  
  Peddy recommends flagging samples when predicted sex, relatedness, or 1000 Genomes-based ancestry inference disagrees with stated metadata, treating such discordance as evidence of sample swaps, mislabeling, contamination, or unintended individuals that should trigger remediation before downstream genetic discovery analyses proceed.
- **[Tukiainen 2017]** Tukiainen et al. (2017). Landscape of X chromosome inactivation across human tissues. *Nature*. [doi:10.1038/nature24265](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature24265)  
  Analysis of 5,500+ GTEx transcriptomes (449 individuals, 29 tissues) plus 940 single-cell transcriptomes shows XCI at 683 X-linked genes is largely tissue-uniform; incomplete XCI affects ≥23% of these genes, with seven genes showing strong multi-evidence support for escape and sex-biased expression.
- **[Thompson 2019]** Thompson et al. (2019). Genetic predisposition to mosaic Y chromosome loss in blood. *Nature*. [doi:10.1038/s41586-019-1765-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1765-3)  
  Using a computational approach on UK Biobank data (n=205,011), the study found ~20% of men have detectable mosaic Y chromosome loss (LOY) and identified 156 autosomal genetic loci associated with LOY, replicated across 757,114 European and Japanese men.
- **[Webster 2019]** Webster et al. (2019). Identifying, understanding, and correcting technical artifacts on the sex chromosomes in next-generation sequencing data. *GigaScience*. [doi:10.1093/gigascience/giz074](https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/giz074)  
  Sequence homology between X and Y chromosomes causes read mismapping that degrades variant calling accuracy; XYalign corrects this mismapping, improving variant calls, and its output metrics (mapping quality, depth, allele balance) can reliably infer sex chromosome complement (XX vs. XY) across low-/high-coverage WGS and exome data.
- **[Godfrey 2020]** Godfrey et al. (2020). Quantitative analysis of Y-Chromosome gene expression across 36 human tissues. *Genome Research*. [doi:10.1101/gr.261248.120](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.261248.120)  
  Loss of a Y-linked microRNA target site drove EIF1AY (but not EIF1AX) up-regulation specifically in heart tissue, yielding EIF1AY protein levels nearly twice as high in male versus female heart—demonstrating X/Y regulatory-sequence divergence can produce tissue-specific, Y-linked sex biases in dosage-sensitive gene expression.
- **[Oliva 2020]** Oliva et al. (2020). The impact of sex on gene expression across human tissues. *Science*. [doi:10.1126/science.aba3066](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aba3066)  
  Across 44 GTEx v8 tissues, 37% of genes show sex-biased expression in at least one tissue; integrating sex-differentiated cis-eQTLs with GWAS data identified 58 gene-trait associations driven by single-sex genetic regulation of expression.
- **[Olney 2020]** Olney et al. (2020). Reference genome and transcriptome informed by the sex chromosome complement of the sample increase ability to detect sex differences in gene expression from RNA-Seq data. *Biology of Sex Differences*. [doi:10.1186/s13293-020-00312-9](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13293-020-00312-9)  
  Using sex-chromosome-complement-informed reference genomes/transcriptomes (Y-masked for XX samples; PAR-masked on Y for XY samples) increases pseudoautosomal-region expression estimates and detects more sex-differentially-expressed genes than default GRCh38 alignment, regardless of aligner (HISAT/STAR); Salmon pseudo-alignment with informed references eliminates spurious Y-linked expression calls in XX samples.
- **[Pedersen 2020]** Pedersen et al. (2020). Somalier: rapid relatedness estimation for cancer and germline studies using efficient genome sketches. *Genome Medicine*. [doi:10.1186/s13073-020-00761-2](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13073-020-00761-2)  
  Somalier extracts small genome sketches from alignments to enable sub-second pairwise relatedness comparisons across hundreds of samples, without requiring joint germline variant calling. It successfully detects sample swaps and relatedness in glioma (normal/tumor/cfDNA), 1000 Genomes, and GTEx (WGS vs RNA-seq) datasets, across diverse sequencing types and genome builds.
- **[Dumanski 2021]** Dumanski et al. (2021). Immune cells lacking Y chromosome show dysregulation of autosomal gene expression. *Cellular and Molecular Life Sciences*. [doi:10.1007/s00018-021-03822-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00018-021-03822-w)

- **[Sano 2022]** Sano et al. (2022). Hematopoietic loss of Y chromosome leads to cardiac fibrosis and heart failure mortality. *Science*. [doi:10.1126/science.abn3100](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abn3100)  
  Hematopoietic mosaic loss of Y chromosome (mLOY) causally drives cardiac fibrosis, reduced cardiac function, and increased mortality in mice, via macrophage polarization toward a fibrotic phenotype reversible by TGFβ1-neutralizing antibody treatment; in a prospective human study, blood mLOY predicted elevated cardiovascular disease and heart failure-associated mortality risk.
- **[Vermeulen 2022]** Vermeulen et al. (2022). Mosaic loss of Chromosome Y in aged human microglia. *Genome Research*. [doi:10.1101/gr.276409.121](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.276409.121)  
  Across 851,674 brain cells, LOY was enriched specifically in microglia (rare in neurons, astrocytes, oligodendrocytes) and significantly more frequent in Alzheimer's disease subjects; microglial LOY was associated with differential expression of 172 autosomal, 3 X-linked, and 10 pseudoautosomal genes.

## Sample identity and duplicates (`SAMPLE_IDENTITY_GUIDELINES.md`)

### Fingerprinting, mix-ups, contamination and demultiplexing

- **[Westra 2011]** Westra et al. (2011). MixupMapper: correcting sample mix-ups in genome-wide datasets increases power to detect small genetic effects. *Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btr323](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btr323)  
  MixupMapper detected sample mix-ups averaging 3% of samples across five datasets (up to 23% in one), and correcting them increased significant cis-eQTL detection by an average of 15% (up to 3-fold in one dataset); uncorrected mix-ups can also underestimate heritability estimates in GWAS data.
- **[Jun 2012]** Jun et al. (2012). Detecting and Estimating Contamination of Human DNA Samples in Sequencing and Array-Based Genotype Data. *The American Journal of Human Genetics*. [doi:10.1016/j.ajhg.2012.09.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajhg.2012.09.004)  
  Methods combining sequencing reads with array genotypes, sequencing reads alone, or array genotypes alone can reliably detect and estimate within-species DNA contamination as low as 1%; guidelines should recommend pre-sequencing array-based screening and sequence-read-based checks prior to variant calling to prevent genotype misclassification and false-positive associations.
- **[Broman 2015]** Broman et al. (2015). Identification and Correction of Sample Mix-Ups in Expression Genetic Data: A Case Study. *G3 Genes|Genomes|Genetics*. [doi:10.1534/g3.115.019778](https://doi.org/10.1534/g3.115.019778)  
  Found 18% genotype sample mix-ups in a 500+ mouse intercross using local eQTL-based genotype prediction from expression data across six tissues; errors traced largely to off-by-one/off-by-two plate pipetting mistakes, with methods implemented in the R package R/lineup for detection and correction.
- **[Bergmann 2016]** Bergmann et al. (2016). Conpair: concordance and contamination estimator for matched tumor–normal pairs. *Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btw389](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btw389)  
  Conpair reliably detects cross-sample contamination as low as 0.1% in tumor-normal sequencing data, even with copy number alterations present, and contamination levels correlate strongly with tumor-normal concordance and the number of germline variants misclassified as somatic by common somatic callers.
- **[Wang 2016]** Wang et al. (2016). BAM-matcher: a tool for rapid NGS sample matching. *Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btw239](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btw239)  
  BAM-matcher determines whether two BAM files originate from the same biological source by comparing genotype calls directly from sequence data, eliminating the need for independent high-density SNP genotyping to detect sample mislabelling—particularly useful for verifying matched tumor/normal pairs early in sequencing pipelines.
- **[Kang 2017]** Kang et al. (2017). Multiplexed droplet single-cell RNA-sequencing using natural genetic variation. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/nbt.4042](https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt.4042)  
  Demuxlet requires only 50 SNPs per cell to correctly assign 97% of singlets and detect 92% of doublets across pools of up to 64 individuals; with real genotyping data on 8 pooled samples it achieves >99% singlet assignment accuracy and consistent doublet detection rates.
- **[Lee 2017]** Lee et al. (2017). NGSCheckMate: software for validating sample identity in next-generation sequencing studies within and across data types. *Nucleic Acids Research*. [doi:10.1093/nar/gkx193](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkx193)  
  NGSCheckMate reliably matches same-subject samples across data types (exome, WGS, RNA-seq, ChIP-seq, targeted, single-cell WGS) using model-based SNP allele-fraction comparison, working at depths as low as >0.5X; recommend running it as a routine QC step, with an alignment-free FASTQ module available for rapid initial checks.
- **[Huang 2019]** Huang et al. (2019). Vireo: Bayesian demultiplexing of pooled single-cell RNA-seq data without genotype reference. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-019-1865-2](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-019-1865-2)  
  Vireo robustly demultiplexes pooled scRNA-seq using genetic variants even with partial or no genotype reference, performing comparably to full-genotype methods on synthetic mixtures and real data—recommending its use when complete genotype data for pooled samples is unavailable.
- **[Heaton 2020]** Heaton et al. (2020). Souporcell: robust clustering of single-cell RNA-seq data by genotype without reference genotypes. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/s41592-020-0820-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-020-0820-1)  
  Souporcell clusters scRNA-seq reads by genetic variants to assign cells to donor of origin without reference genotypes, detecting cross-genotype doublets invisible to transcriptional methods and estimating ambient RNA contamination levels; it demonstrates high accuracy across challenging multiplexed donor-mixing scenarios for clustering, doublet detection, and ambient RNA estimation.
- **[Javed 2020]** Javed et al. (2020). Detecting sample swaps in diverse NGS data types using linkage disequilibrium. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/s41467-020-17453-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-17453-5)  
  Crosscheck (using linkage-disequilibrium-based fingerprinting) outperforms comparable sample-matching methods and remains effective on sparse or cross-assay data; applied to 8851 ENCODE ChIP-, RNA-, and DNase-seq datasets, it identified and corrected dozens of mislabeled samples/metadata errors, representing ~1% of datasets.
- **[Wang 2022]** Wang et al. (2022). Doppelgänger spotting in biomedical gene expression data. *iScience*. [doi:10.1016/j.isci.2022.104788](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2022.104788)  
  Doppelgänger effects—chance cross-set sample similarities inflating ML performance estimates—are pervasive across diverse gene expression datasets/diseases; residual batch effects from batch imbalances reduce detection sensitivity, so analysts should run doppelgängerIdentifier-based verification before training to establish realistic performance baselines and judge whether feature selection/ML results are meaningful.
- **[Cardiello 2023]** Cardiello et al. (2023). Evaluation of genetic demultiplexing of single-cell sequencing data from model species. *Life Science Alliance*. [doi:10.26508/lsa.202301979](https://doi.org/10.26508/lsa.202301979)  
  Genotype-based demultiplexing (e.g., using de novo transcriptomes without reference genomes) performs reliably across non-isogenic model organisms from zebrafish to non-human primates, but has species-specific failure modes; guidelines should recommend it for pooled sc-seq study designs to cut costs and improve replicate reproducibility while noting its limitations.
- **[Neavin 2024]** Neavin et al. (2024). Demuxafy: improvement in droplet assignment by integrating multiple single-cell demultiplexing and doublet detection methods. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-024-03224-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-024-03224-8)  
  Demuxafy improves droplet assignment accuracy by combining results from multiple demultiplexing and doublet-detection tools via consensus intersection, outperforming any single method in correctly separating singlets from doublets and assigning cells to the correct donor—though the abstract provides no specific performance thresholds or numeric benchmarks.

## Deconvolution (`DECONVOLUTION_GUIDELINES.md`)

### Benchmarks, methods and interpretation

- **[Newman 2015]** Newman et al. (2015). Robust enumeration of cell subsets from tissue expression profiles. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/nmeth.3337](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.3337)  
  CIBERSORT, a support vector regression-based deconvolution method, outperformed existing approaches for estimating immune cell subsets from bulk tissue expression data—including fresh, frozen, and fixed samples and solid tumors—showing greater robustness to noise, unknown mixture content, and closely related cell types.
- **[Racle 2017]** Racle et al. (2017). Simultaneous enumeration of cancer and immune cell types from bulk tumor gene expression data. *eLife*. [doi:10.7554/elife.26476](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.26476)  
  EPIC, validated against flow cytometry, immunohistochemistry, and single-cell RNA-Seq in melanoma and colorectal tumors, improves accuracy over existing methods by incorporating cell-type-specific mRNA content renormalization and accounting for uncharacterized/variable cell populations, enabling absolute (not just relative) cancer and immune cell fraction estimates from bulk tumor expression data.
- **[Monaco 2019]** Monaco et al. (2019). RNA-Seq Signatures Normalized by mRNA Abundance Allow Absolute Deconvolution of Human Immune Cell Types. *Cell Reports*. [doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2019.01.041](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2019.01.041)  
  The study profiled 29 PBMC immune cell types by RNA-seq and flow cytometry, derived cell-type-specific/housekeeping gene signatures normalized by mRNA abundance, and benchmarked deconvolution/normalization methods to enable absolute (not just relative) immune cell proportion estimation from bulk transcriptomics, implemented in the ABIS Shiny app.
- **[Newman 2019]** Newman et al. (2019). Determining cell type abundance and expression from bulk tissues with digital cytometry. *Nature Biotechnology*. [doi:10.1038/s41587-019-0114-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-019-0114-2)  
  CIBERSORTx infers cell-type-specific gene expression from bulk tissue without physical cell isolation by correcting platform-specific technical variation, enabling single-cell RNA-seq references to deconvolve bulk tumor transcriptomes (e.g., melanoma), linking cell-type-specific phenotypic states to driver mutations and immune checkpoint blockade response.
- **[Sturm 2019]** Sturm et al. (2019). Comprehensive evaluation of transcriptome-based cell-type quantification methods for immuno-oncology. *Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btz363](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btz363)  
  Computational deconvolution achieves high accuracy for well-defined immune/stromal cell-type signatures but fails for cell types with "fuzzy" signatures; across seven methods benchmarked on nine cell types (~1800 samples), the authors recommend refining cell-population definitions and improving signature specificity rather than endorsing a single universally best tool.
- **[Tsoucas 2019]** Tsoucas et al. (2019). Accurate estimation of cell-type composition from gene expression data. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/s41467-019-10802-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-10802-z)  
  A novel single-cell RNA-seq-signature-based deconvolution algorithm outperforms existing methods in estimating bulk RNA-seq cell-type composition, particularly for rare cell types, and can detect composition shifts from drug treatments or condition changes—recommended for cost-effective cell-type-specific analysis of perturbation studies.
- **[Wang 2019]** Wang et al. (2019). Bulk tissue cell type deconvolution with multi-subject single-cell expression reference. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/s41467-018-08023-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-08023-x)  
  MuSiC, which weights genes by cross-subject and cross-cell-type consistency to transfer cell-type-specific expression from scRNA-seq to bulk RNA-seq deconvolution, outperformed existing methods on pancreatic islet and whole kidney data (human, mouse, rat), with particular advantage when distinguishing closely related cell types.
- **[Avila Cobos 2020]** Avila Cobos et al. (2020). Benchmarking of cell type deconvolution pipelines for transcriptomics data. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/s41467-020-19015-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-19015-1)  
  For cell type deconvolution of bulk transcriptomics data, use linear-scale (non-log) expression and ensure the reference includes all cell types present in the mixture to avoid substantially worse accuracy.
- **[Dong 2020]** Dong et al. (2020). SCDC: bulk gene expression deconvolution by multiple single-cell RNA sequencing references. *Briefings in Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bib/bbz166](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbz166)  
  SCDC outperforms existing bulk RNA-seq deconvolution methods on both pseudo-bulk and experimentally mixed cell-line benchmarks; its ENSEMBLE approach, combining multiple scRNA-seq references, mitigates cross-study batch effects and yields cell-type proportion estimates more consistent with known experimental designs in pancreatic islet and mammary gland tissue applications.
- **[Jew 2020]** Jew et al. (2020). Accurate estimation of cell composition in bulk expression through robust integration of single-cell information. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/s41467-020-15816-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-15816-6)  
  Bisque's regression-based, gene-specific transformations outperform existing decomposition methods when technical variation exists between reference (scRNA-seq/snRNA-seq) and bulk RNA-seq data, is computationally efficient for large datasets, and accurately recovers cell-proportion associations even for rare cell types; it also supports a marker-gene-only mode requiring no full reference profile.
- **[Menden 2020]** Menden et al. (2020). Deep learning–based cell composition analysis from tissue expression profiles. *Science Advances*. [doi:10.1126/sciadv.aba2619](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aba2619)  
  Scaden, a deep neural network trained on single-cell RNA-seq data, outperforms existing deconvolution algorithms in precision and robustness, generalizing across bulk RNA-seq, microarray, human, and mouse tissue data using a single trained network without requiring dataset-specific preprocessing or feature selection.
- **[Patrick 2020]** Patrick et al. (2020). Deconvolving the contributions of cell-type heterogeneity on cortical gene expression. *PLOS Computational Biology*. [doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008120](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008120)  
  Deconvolution algorithms applied to bulk cortical expression, using marker sets from brain single-cell/sorted RNA-seq, produce IHC-validated cell-type proportion estimates (including neuronal subtypes); guidelines should include these estimates as confounders to reduce false Alzheimer's-expression associations and use accurate marker sets to boost cell-type-specific eQTL detection power.
- **[Jin 2021]** Jin & Liu (2021). A benchmark for RNA-seq deconvolution analysis under dynamic testing environments. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-021-02290-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-021-02290-6)  
  The abstract reports a benchmark of 11 deconvolution methods across 1766 conditions, testing sensitivity to simulation model, quantification unit, component number, weight matrix, and unknown content—but does not specify which method performed best or any numeric thresholds, so no concrete method recommendation or failure-mode threshold can be cited from it.
- **[Sosina 2021]** Sosina et al. (2021). Strategies for cellular deconvolution in human brain RNA sequencing data. *F1000Research*. [doi:10.12688/f1000research.50858.1](https://doi.org/10.12688/f1000research.50858.1)  
  Houseman, MuSiC, and NNLS gave concordant but biased neuronal fraction estimates (R²=0.51–0.56, RMSE>0.35) with mismatched scRNA-seq references, while CIBERSORT was less biased (RMSE=0.09) but more discordant (R²=0.25); even matched snRNA-seq references didn't eliminate bias (MuSiC RMSE=0.17), indicating these methods estimate RNA composition, not true cellular compos
- **[Chen 2022]** Chen et al. (2022). Deep autoencoder for interpretable tissue-adaptive deconvolution and cell-type-specific gene analysis. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/s41467-022-34550-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-34550-9)  
  TAPE, a deep autoencoder connecting scRNA-seq and bulk RNA-seq, outperforms existing deconvolution methods overall, achieves comparable cell-type-level accuracy, and is more robust across cell types, faster, and better able to recover biologically meaningful cell-type-specific gene expression from clinical bulk data.
- **[Chu 2022]** Chu et al. (2022). Cell type and gene expression deconvolution with BayesPrism enables Bayesian integrative analysis across bulk and single-cell RNA sequencing in oncology. *Nature Cancer*. [doi:10.1038/s43018-022-00356-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43018-022-00356-3)  
  BayesPrism, using patient-derived scRNA-seq priors, outperforms existing deconvolution approaches for inferring cell-type composition and cell-type-specific gene expression from bulk RNA-seq in glioblastoma, head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, and melanoma, enabling refined tumor subtyping after excluding confounding nonmalignant cell contributions and identification of malignant-cell genes correlating with immune/stromal infiltration.
- **[Fan 2022]** Fan et al. (2022). MuSiC2: cell-type deconvolution for multi-condition bulk RNA-seq data. *Briefings in Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bib/bbac430](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbac430)  
  MuSiC2 (iterative extension of MuSiC) outperforms standard MuSiC deconvolution when bulk RNA-seq samples span multiple clinical conditions not matched to the scRNA-seq reference, yielding more accurate cell-type proportion estimates; validated on human pancreatic islet and retina bulk RNA-seq datasets, recommending its use whenever reference and target disease conditions differ.
- **[Sutton 2022]** Sutton et al. (2022). Comprehensive evaluation of deconvolution methods for human brain gene expression. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/s41467-022-28655-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-28655-4)  
  Based on the abstract alone, specific thresholds or a single best-performing method/signature are not stated; the paper's citable findings are that deconvolution accuracy for brain tissue depends strongly on biological factors—particularly brain region specificity and in vitro cell culturing effects on cell-type signatures—rather than being tissue-generic, unlike pancreas or heart.
- **[Avila Cobos 2023]** Avila Cobos et al. (2023). Effective methods for bulk RNA-seq deconvolution using scnRNA-seq transcriptomes. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-023-03016-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-023-03016-6)  
  SQUID (combining RNA-seq transformation with dampened weighted least-squares deconvolution) consistently outperformed other methods for estimating cell-type composition from bulk RNA-seq using scRNA-seq/snRNA-seq references; concurrent RNA-seq and scnRNA-seq profiling improves preprocessing and deconvolution accuracy, which proved necessary for detecting outcome-predictive cancer subclones in pediatric AML and neuroblastoma.
- **[Hu 2024]** Hu & Chikina (2024). InstaPrism: an R package for fast implementation of BayesPrism. *Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btae440](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btae440)  
  InstaPrism reproduces BayesPrism's cell-type deconvolution results with effectively equivalent accuracy by replacing Gibbs sampling with a derandomized fixed-point algorithm, while offering substantial speed and memory savings, and provides precompiled curated reference sets tailored to multiple cancer types for streamlined deconvolution.
- **[Huuki-Myers 2025]** Huuki-Myers et al. (2025). Benchmark of cellular deconvolution methods using a multi-assay dataset from postmortem human prefrontal cortex. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-025-03552-3](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-025-03552-3)  
  Bisque and hspe were the most accurate deconvolution methods for estimating cell type proportions from bulk RNA-seq using snRNA-seq reference data, validated against RNAScope/immunofluorescence in human prefrontal cortex; the Mean Ratio marker-gene selection method and benchmark dataset are available via the DeconvoBuddies R/Bioconductor package.
- **[Singh 2026]** Singh et al. (2026). Benchmarking cell-type deconvolution in cross-platform transcriptomic data. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-026-04222-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-026-04222-8)  
  SpatialDecon and cell2location showed the most reliable, consistent deconvolution accuracy across both simulated and real cross-platform transcriptomic datasets, outperforming other tools under varying platform-specific technical biases; method choice should be guided by experimental design (platform type, technological characteristics) rather than applied uniformly across datasets.

## Meta-analysis (`META_ANALYSIS_GUIDELINES.md`)

### General methodology

- **[DerSimonian 1986]** DerSimonian & Laird (1986). Meta-analysis in clinical trials. *Controlled Clinical Trials*. [doi:10.1016/0197-2456(86)90046-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/0197-2456(86)90046-2)  
  Meta-analyses should use a random effects model incorporating between-study heterogeneity, with a simple noniterative procedure for estimating the distribution of treatment effects before pooling trial results.
- **[Egger 1997]** Egger et al. (1997). Bias in meta-analysis detected by a simple, graphical test. *BMJ*. [doi:10.1136/bmj.315.7109.629](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.315.7109.629)  
  A simple linear regression test of funnel plot asymmetry (standard normal deviates vs. precision) can detect likely bias in meta-analyses, but results require cautious interpretation when based on few small trials.
- **[Higgins 2002]** Higgins & Thompson (2002). Quantifying heterogeneity in a meta‐analysis. *Statistics in Medicine*. [doi:10.1002/sim.1186](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.1186)  
  Meta-analyses should report the heterogeneity statistics H and/or I², which quantify the impact of heterogeneity independent of study number and effect metric, rather than relying solely on the heterogeneity test.
- **[Higgins 2003]** Higgins et al. (2003). Measuring inconsistency in meta-analyses. *BMJ*. [doi:10.1136/bmj.327.7414.557](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.327.7414.557)  
  Introduces $I^2$: percentage of total variation across studies due to heterogeneity rather than chance.
- **[Viechtbauer 2010]** Viechtbauer (2010). Conducting Meta-Analyses in R with the metafor Package. *Journal of Statistical Software*. [doi:10.18637/jss.v036.i03](https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v036.i03)  
  metafor: R reference implementation for fixed-, random- and mixed-effects meta-analysis and meta-regression, with REML and Knapp-Hartung options.
- **[Riley 2011]** Riley et al. (2011). Interpretation of random effects meta-analyses. *BMJ*. [doi:10.1136/bmj.d549](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.d549)  
  Random-effects results must be interpreted as an average effect; report a prediction interval for the effect in a new setting.
- **[Sterne 2011]** Sterne et al. (2011). Recommendations for examining and interpreting funnel plot asymmetry in meta-analyses of randomised controlled trials. *BMJ*. [doi:10.1136/bmj.d4002](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.d4002)  
  Funnel-plot asymmetry tests need at least 10 studies; asymmetry has causes other than publication bias (heterogeneity, chance, small-study effects).
- **[IntHout 2014]** IntHout et al. (2014). The Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman method for random effects meta-analysis is straightforward and considerably outperforms the standard DerSimonian-Laird method. *BMC Medical Research Methodology*. [doi:10.1186/1471-2288-14-25](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2288-14-25)  
  Meta-analyses using random effects models should apply the Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman method rather than DerSimonian-Laird, as it yields more adequate error rates, particularly with few or unequally sized studies.
- **[Röver 2015]** Röver et al. (2015). Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman approach and its modification for random-effects meta-analysis with few studies. *BMC Medical Research Methodology*. [doi:10.1186/s12874-015-0091-1](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12874-015-0091-1)  
  When few studies with varying standard errors are meta-analyzed, use the modified Knapp-Hartung (mKH) correction rather than standard HKSJ, as it yields more conservative, nominal-level-accurate confidence intervals.
- **[Veroniki 2015]** Veroniki et al. (2015). Methods to estimate the between‐study variance and its uncertainty in meta‐analysis. *Research Synthesis Methods*. [doi:10.1002/jrsm.1164](https://doi.org/10.1002/jrsm.1164)  
  For estimating between-study variance in meta-analysis, use the Paule-Mandel (or REML for continuous data) estimator with Q-profile or generalised Cochran confidence intervals rather than DerSimonian-Laird.
- **[Langan 2018]** Langan et al. (2018). A comparison of heterogeneity variance estimators in simulated random‐effects meta‐analyses. *Research Synthesis Methods*. [doi:10.1002/jrsm.1316](https://doi.org/10.1002/jrsm.1316)  
  For random-effects meta-analyses, especially with few or unequal-sized studies, use REML for heterogeneity variance estimation and the Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman method for robust confidence intervals.
- **[Balduzzi 2019]** Balduzzi et al. (2019). How to perform a meta-analysis with R: a practical tutorial. *Evidence Based Mental Health*. [doi:10.1136/ebmental-2019-300117](https://doi.org/10.1136/ebmental-2019-300117)  
  The R packages meta and metasens enable researchers to perform fixed/random effects meta-analysis, subgroup analysis, forest/funnel plots, and adjust for funnel plot asymmetry and missing-data bias.
- **[Sterne 2019]** Sterne et al. (2019). RoB 2: a revised tool for assessing risk of bias in randomised trials. *BMJ*. [doi:10.1136/bmj.l4898](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l4898)  
  RoB 2: structured tool to assess risk of bias in randomised trials across five domains.
- **[Mathur 2020]** Mathur & VanderWeele (2020). Sensitivity Analysis for Publication Bias in Meta-Analyses. *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series C: Applied Statistics*. [doi:10.1111/rssc.12440](https://doi.org/10.1111/rssc.12440)  
  The PublicationBias R package performs sensitivity analyses quantifying how many-fold more likely "significant" results must be published to explain away a meta-analysis's findings, including a worst-case estimate using only non-significant studies.
- **[Nakagawa 2021]** Nakagawa et al. (2021). Methods for testing publication bias in ecological and evolutionary meta‐analyses. *Methods in Ecology and Evolution*. [doi:10.1111/2041-210x.13724](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210x.13724)  
  Most publication-bias tests ignore heterogeneity and non-independence among effect sizes; proposes multilevel meta-regression to test small-study and time-lag effects.
- **[Page 2021]** Page et al. (2021). The PRISMA 2020 statement: an updated guideline for reporting systematic reviews. *BMJ*. [doi:10.1136/bmj.n71](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.n71)  
  PRISMA 2020: 27-item checklist and flow diagram for reporting systematic reviews and meta-analyses.
- **[Bartoš 2022]** Bartoš et al. (2022). Robust Bayesian meta‐analysis: Model‐averaging across complementary publication bias adjustment methods. *Research Synthesis Methods*. [doi:10.1002/jrsm.1594](https://doi.org/10.1002/jrsm.1594)  
  Robust Bayesian model-averaging across selection models and small-study-effect models for meta-analysis adjusts for publication bias without forcing an all-or-none choice between conflicting correction methods.
- **[Afonso 2023]** Afonso et al. (2023). The Perils of Misinterpreting and Misusing “Publication Bias” in Meta-analyses: An Education Review on Funnel Plot-Based Methods. *Sports Medicine*. [doi:10.1007/s40279-023-01927-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-023-01927-9)  
  Because funnel-plot methods for detecting publication bias are often underpowered and confounded by heterogeneity, reviews should report "risk of publication bias" and prioritize preventive strategies like pre-registration.
- **[Borenstein 2023]** Borenstein (2023). Avoiding common mistakes in meta‐analysis: Understanding the distinct roles of Q , I ‐squared, tau‐squared, and the prediction interval in reporting heterogeneity. *Research Synthesis Methods*. [doi:10.1002/jrsm.1678](https://doi.org/10.1002/jrsm.1678)  
  Meta-analyses should report the prediction interval—not just Q, p-values, or I²—because only it quantifies the actual range of true effect sizes across populations, revealing whether effects are consistently moderate or highly variable.

### Omics and single-cell meta-analysis

- **[Hong 2006]** Hong et al. (2006). RankProd: a bioconductor package for detecting differentially expressed genes in meta-analysis. *Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btl476](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btl476)  
  RankProd is a Bioconductor package that detects differentially expressed genes across multiple microarray studies by extending the rank product method, providing permutation-based P-values and FDR estimates.
- **[Ramasamy 2008]** Ramasamy et al. (2008). Key Issues in Conducting a Meta-Analysis of Gene Expression Microarray Datasets. *PLoS Medicine*. [doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.0050184](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.0050184)  
  Practical step-by-step guide for meta-analysis of gene expression datasets: dataset identification, extraction, preprocessing and annotation, method choice, interpretation.
- **[Tseng 2012]** Tseng et al. (2012). Comprehensive literature review and statistical considerations for microarray meta-analysis. *Nucleic Acids Research*. [doi:10.1093/nar/gkr1265](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkr1265)  
  Based on a systematic review of 333 microarray meta-analysis papers, researchers should carefully scrutinize statistical procedures, databases, and software choices, guided by purpose-specific considerations illustrated through a case study.
- **[Rau 2014]** Rau et al. (2014). Differential meta-analysis of RNA-seq data from multiple studies. *BMC Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1186/1471-2105-15-91](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-15-91)  
  metaRNASeq implements p-value combination methods for RNA-seq meta-analysis across studies, outperforming a fixed-effect negative binomial GLM when inter-study variability or study numbers are moderate to large.
- **[Sweeney 2016]** Sweeney et al. (2016). Methods to increase reproducibility in differential gene expression via meta-analysis. *Nucleic Acids Research*. [doi:10.1093/nar/gkw797](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkw797)  
  Gene expression meta-analysis reproducibility improves by using more datasets (not just larger sample size), stringent effect-size thresholds with relaxed significance cutoffs, and avoiding unnecessary heterogeneity constraints.
- **[Crow 2018]** Crow et al. (2018). Characterizing the replicability of cell types defined by single cell RNA-sequencing data using MetaNeighbor. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/s41467-018-03282-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-03282-0)  
  MetaNeighbor quantifies cell-type replicability across scRNA-seq datasets using variably expressed gene sets, enabling identification of robust, high-accuracy replicable cell types and candidate marker genes despite technical and naming inconsistencies.
- **[Wilson 2019]** Wilson (2019). The harmonic mean p -value for combining dependent tests. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*. [doi:10.1073/pnas.1814092116](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1814092116)  
  The harmonic mean p-value combines dependent tests to control the familywise error rate while improving statistical power, enabling detection of significant hypothesis groups otherwise missed by individual testing.
- **[Toro-Domínguez 2020]** Toro-Domínguez et al. (2020). A survey of gene expression meta-analysis: methods and applications. *Briefings in Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bib/bbaa019](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbaa019)  
  This survey outlines the key steps, statistical methods, and software tools for gene expression meta-analysis, providing a practical guide for selecting the most appropriate method per application.
- **[Nguyen 2023]** Nguyen et al. (2023). Benchmarking integration of single-cell differential expression. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/s41467-023-37126-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-37126-3)  
  For single-cell differential expression across batches, use uncorrected data with limmatrend, Wilcoxon test, or fixed effects models, especially for sparse or low-depth data.
- **[Nakatsuka 2025]** Nakatsuka et al. (2025). Improving reproducibility of differentially expressed genes in single-cell transcriptomic studies of neurodegenerative diseases through meta-analysis. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/s41467-025-62579-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-62579-z)  
  SumRank, a non-parametric meta-analysis method ranking reproducibility of relative differential expression across scRNA-seq datasets, identifies DEGs with higher specificity and sensitivity than dataset-merging or p-value aggregation approaches.

### Public data resources

- **[Lachmann 2018]** Lachmann et al. (2018). Massive mining of publicly available RNA-seq data from human and mouse. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/s41467-018-03751-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-03751-6)  
  ARCHS4 is a web resource that uniformly aligns and provides public human and mouse RNA-seq data from GEO at gene/transcript levels, with co-expression and functional annotation tools.
- **[Wilks 2021]** Wilks et al. (2021). recount3: summaries and queries for large-scale RNA-seq expression and splicing. *Genome Biology*. [doi:10.1186/s13059-021-02533-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-021-02533-6)  
  recount3 provides uniformly processed expression and splicing summaries for over 750,000 human and mouse RNA-seq samples, with R/Bioconductor tools enabling direct comparison to locally processed private data via the Monorail pipeline.
- **[CZI 2024]** CZI Cell Science Program (2024). CZ CELLxGENE Discover: a single-cell data platform for scalable exploration, analysis and modeling of aggregated data. *Nucleic Acids Research*. [doi:10.1093/nar/gkae1142](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkae1142)  
  CZ CELLxGENE Discover is a free online platform providing curated, standardized, interoperable single-cell transcriptomic data (93+ million cells) with tools for cross-corpus and meta-analysis at single-cell resolution.
